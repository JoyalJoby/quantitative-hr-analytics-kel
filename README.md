## Abstract

Organizations increasingly recognize that sustainable performance depends on a motivated
workforce rather than on capital and process efficiency alone. Building on the foundational
work of Mayo's Hawthorne Studies and classical motivation theories (Maslow, Herzberg), this
project presents a quantitative, survey-based assessment of employee motivation and its
relationship to job satisfaction and perceived performance at Kerala Electrical and Allied
Engineering Co. Ltd. (KEL). Primary data were collected from 100 employees across 20 structured
survey items covering compensation, recognition, working conditions, career development, and
management relations. Exploratory data analysis, cross-tabulation, and chi-square tests of
independence are used to characterize the satisfaction landscape and test associations between
perceived motivation impact and related HR outcomes. The findings indicate generally positive
sentiment toward working conditions, coworker relationships, and colleague support, alongside
comparatively lower satisfaction with promotion opportunities and monetary incentives —
suggesting that non-financial, relational dimensions of the workplace currently outperform
career-progression and compensation dimensions in driving employee sentiment at KEL.

## 1. Background and Motivation

Employee motivation has long been established as a determinant of organizational productivity,
dating back to Elton Mayo's Hawthorne Studies (1924–1932), which repositioned workers from
passive units of production to active contributors whose psychological and social needs shape
output. This study operationalizes that theoretical tradition into a structured survey instrument
administered at KEL, a regional electrical and allied-engineering manufacturer, to empirically
assess the state of employee motivation and its perceived link to performance.

## 2. Research Objectives

**Primary objective**
- To study the effectiveness of employee motivation and performance in the organization.

**Secondary objectives**
- To identify the most important factors influencing employee motivation and performance at work.
- To examine the relationship between employee motivation and job satisfaction.
- To examine the relationship between employee motivation and performance.

## 3. Data

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Respondents | 100 employees of KEL |
| Variables | 20 (all ordinal/categorical, Likert-style or frequency scales) |
| Collection method | Structured questionnaire, primary data |
| Collection window | 1–29 February 2024 |
| Format | `data/kel_employee_survey_data.csv` |

Variables span four broad domains: **compensation & recognition** (salary, monetary incentives,
recognition), **work environment** (working conditions, coworker relationships, colleague
support), **career development** (promotion opportunities, professional development, training,
performance appraisal), and **perceived drivers of performance** (qualification, experience, and
motivation impact).

### Limitations
- One-month data collection window constrained sample depth in some departments (notably production).
- Convenience/availability-based response from a busy workforce; some respondents were less cooperative.
- Self-reported perceptions, which are subject to social-desirability and recall bias.

## 4. Methodology

All analysis is contained in `notebooks/01_exploratory_data_analysis.ipynb` and uses:

- **pandas** for data loading, cleaning, and ordinal encoding
- **seaborn / matplotlib** for univariate and bivariate visualization
- **scipy.stats.chi2_contingency** for chi-square tests of independence between perceived
  motivation impact and related HR outcomes

Each ordinal survey response was mapped to a 1–5 numeric score (1 = most negative / least
frequent, 5 = most positive / most frequent) to enable ranking of mean satisfaction across
dimensions, without assuming the categories are more than ordinal in nature — the numeric
scores are used descriptively (mean ranking, correlation) rather than as a validated
interval-level construct.

## 5. Key Findings

**Sample profile**
- 64% of respondents have 1–5 years of experience, 16% have 5–10 years, 12% have 10–15 years,
  and 8% have more than 15 years — a relatively junior-skewed workforce sample.
- 72% of respondents value **both** financial and non-financial incentives; only 12% prioritize
  financial incentives alone.

**Ranked mean satisfaction/influence scores (1–5 scale)**

| Rank | Dimension | Mean score |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coworker relationship satisfaction | 4.00 |
| 2 | Colleague support satisfaction | 3.88 |
| 3 | Nature of work perception | 3.84 |
| 4 | Decision-making participation | 3.80 |
| 5 | Management–employee relationship | 3.76 |
| 5 | Recognition satisfaction | 3.76 |
| 7 | Working conditions satisfaction | 3.68 |
| 8 | Professional development | 3.64 |
| 8 | Supervisor feedback satisfaction | 3.64 |
| 10 | Performance appraisal satisfaction | 3.52 |
| 10 | Job security satisfaction | 3.52 |
| 12 | Training programs satisfaction | 3.40 |

| 14 | Monetary incentives satisfaction | 3.16 |

| 16 | Qualification's influence on performance | 2.20 |

**Interpretation**
- Relational and day-to-day work-environment factors (coworker relationships, colleague
  support, nature of work, participation in decisions) score highest — consistent with the
  ~64–68% positive-sentiment figures reported in the dissertation's findings chapter.
- Compensation-adjacent dimensions (salary, monetary incentives) and career-progression
  dimensions (promotions, training) form a lower-scoring cluster, pointing to these as priority
  areas for intervention.
- Notably, respondents rate **qualification, experience, and motivation itself as having only a
  "minor" influence on performance** on average — suggesting employees attribute performance to
  a diffuse mix of situational and managerial factors rather than to any single individual
  attribute, a nuance not always visible from univariate percentages alone.

**Statistical association tests (χ² test of independence, α = 0.05)**

| Variable pair | χ² | df | p-value | Significant? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motivation impact × Performance appraisal satisfaction | 17.99 | 12 | 0.116 | No |
| Motivation impact × Nature of work perception | 10.44 | 9 | 0.316 | No |
| Motivation impact × Recognition satisfaction | 9.85 | 9 | 0.363 | No |
| Motivation impact × Salary satisfaction | 11.16 | 12 | 0.515 | No |

At this sample size (n = 100) and with 4–5 category variables, none of the tested associations
reach conventional statistical significance, although the motivation–appraisal association is
the strongest of the four (p = 0.116). This suggests that, while the descriptive/percentage-based
findings in the original report point to plausible relationships, they should be interpreted as
directional patterns rather than statistically confirmed effects — a distinction this repository
makes explicit through formal hypothesis testing rather than relying on cross-tab percentages alone.

The strongest pairwise correlations among encoded variables (Pearson's r, all modest in
magnitude, r < 0.22) were recognition satisfaction with nature-of-work perception (r = 0.21) and
monetary incentives satisfaction with working conditions satisfaction (r = 0.21), indicating
weak-to-moderate positive co-movement rather than strong linear dependence — expected given the
categorical/ordinal nature of the underlying constructs.

## 6. Recommendations (from the parent dissertation)

- Increase transparency and frequency of promotion pathways.
- Revisit the performance appraisal system for perceived fairness.
- Strengthen the salary structure and monetary incentive scheme.
- Expand professional development and training program coverage.
- Formalize open communication channels between management and staff.
